# hirdir 0.9.8

Two things. A stack is worked from its rows: a member is replaced in one press, a
switch that stands alone is made a stack from its page, and a spare goes round its
pool as often as it is needed. And a thing that runs on another, a virtual machine on
a hypervisor, a virtual context in a switch, a long-lived container, is an item that
names its host and is where its host is. With them, what the owner answered while
0.9.7 was tested. The schema goes from revision `0012` to `0014` in two migrations,
neither of which changes a record that is held. The API gains routes and members,
listed under Additional Notes.

## New

- **Replace is one press on a member's row.** The row of a current member of a group
  has Replace beside End. Its form, under the row, asks for the item that takes the
  place, offered as the picker of members offers one, with where it stands and the
  spares first; the reason and a reference; in a stack, whether the one that comes
  takes the stack's name and place, a box ticked from the start, with Production
  where it is a spare; and what becomes of the one that left: to the spare pool with a
  new name and a place, to be returned to the manufacturer, excess, or nothing now.
  One request does all of it, and where any part is refused nothing is done. The page
  of an item keeps Replace this item, with the same form. Measured on a scratch
  inventory of 386 stacks and 1,843 fictional switches, where 0.9.7 took some 21
  clicks and 29 keys over two item pages to replace a failed member: 8 clicks and 23
  keys, on the stack's page alone; and both members of a stack of two swapped for
  another model in 22 clicks, where it took 56 over six pages (P-242).
- **An item is replaced, and takes another's place, more than once.** A spare goes
  into a stack for a member that failed, is pulled later and goes back to the pool,
  and takes the place of another; the switch that came back from the manufacturer
  takes the place of the one that had taken its own. 0.9.7 refused both: an item was
  replaced once, and took one place. The page of an item lists its replacements, the
  latest first, each with the other item, the reason, the reference and the day
  (P-241).
- **A switch that stands alone is made a stack from its page.** Make it a stack stands
  beside Join a stack. The stack is given the name the person confirms, the switch's
  own from the start, and takes from the switch what a stack holds for its members:
  the host name and the FQDN, the addresses, each moved as it is, and the location,
  the planned location, the owner and the operator that the switch has of its own,
  which the switch then has from the stack. A second switch may be chosen in the same
  form. Where it took some 35 clicks and 100 keys over four pages: 4 clicks and 12
  keys, on the switch's page alone (P-243).
- **The first members of a new stack are offered the box of a member that joins**, in
  New group, ticked from the start, with Production for a spare (P-244).
- **The condition of a member that leaves is given with the end**, and with its
  replacement: To be returned to the manufacturer starts it at Failed, which the
  person may change (P-245).
- **An Admin removes a replacement that was a mistake**, with a reason, from the line
  of it on the page of either item; the audit trail holds the replacement as it was.
  An item that is part of no replacement any more is deleted by the rule of every
  other (P-238).
- **A thing that runs on another is an item with a host.** A virtual machine on a
  hypervisor, a virtual context in a switch, a Nexus VDC or the like of it in a
  firewall, and a long-lived container are items of their own, each seen on the network
  by its name and its address, and each names what it runs on: an item, or a stack or a
  cluster. It is the reverse of a member of a stack: it keeps its name, its addresses
  and its state, and has its location, its planned location, its owner and its operator
  from its host, resolved when it is read and never copied down, so that moving the
  server moves what runs on it and writes to none of it. Among its first facts its page
  says Runs on, the host as a link, and, where a tool or a person saw it elsewhere,
  Seen on, with when: a machine moves between the hosts of a cluster by itself, so the
  host that is named and the host that was seen are two facts, as the master of a stack
  is. The page of a host lists What runs on it, and what runs on those beneath them.
  Each is a component and a managed node and carries its weight, where its host is
  (P-226, P-246, P-250).
- **What follows from a host.** Retiring an item retires what runs on it, and what runs
  on that, each with its own entry in the audit trail; the page lists them first and
  asks. A stack or a cluster that something runs on is not retired, and says what runs
  on it: each is given another host, or retired, first. One server of a cluster retired
  leaves the cluster's machines with the cluster. A host is not deleted while anything
  names it. Replace, where something runs on the item that leaves, has a box ticked
  from the start, The 3 things that run on it go to the new one (P-247).
- **What runs on a stack runs on the stack, and not on a member of it.** A stack is one
  device: a container on a stack of switches runs on whichever member is active. A
  switch named as a host that is a current member of a stack stands for its stack, and
  the member is what the item was seen on, so that a tool's report, which knows the
  switch and not the stack, still loads. A switch that joins a stack, or is made one,
  hands what runs on it to the stack, and the form says how many before it is
  confirmed. A member that leaves, is replaced or is retired takes nothing with it. It
  holds for a container, a virtual machine and a virtual context alike; a cluster is as
  it was, and a machine runs on the cluster or on one host of it (P-252).
- **The serial number given for a virtual context names its host.** A context has none
  of its own: what a tool reads from it is its switch's. A row of a file that gives one
  finds the switch that has it and makes that switch the context's host; the number is
  not stored on the context, which shows its host's as from its host. A context
  reported before its switch keeps the number it was given and names no host; nothing
  joins the two when the switch is entered; the next report that names the context
  finds the switch and joins them, and until then its page says which item has that
  serial number, with Make it its host. A context moved to another switch shows that
  switch's, with nothing to blank (P-251).
- **A row is found by the tool's own identifier, and what a tool reports no more is
  marked.** An import of items is Read from an external system, and its rows give that
  system's identifier of each thing: a row finds the item registered under it, whatever
  its name is now, so that a rename or a move in the tool makes no second record. With
  This file is everything it reports, what the system is registered with and the file
  does not name is marked Not reported, to be looked at: never retired, and Registered
  again when the next file names it. The preview says how many it would mark and names
  the first of them; a file with a row that was not applied marks nothing and says why
  (P-248).
- **What is to be looked at says so.** An item says why a person should look at it: it
  is of a kind that runs on a host and names none; the host it names is retired or has
  left the team's hands; or a system it is registered with reports it no more. Each is
  a mark, said in words beside what it is about with the way to put it right; it
  refuses nothing and moves nothing. The item list filters and shows it, and the first
  page counts the items to be looked at, by reason, each a link to the list of them
  (P-249).
- **A field that must be filled says so at a glance.** The word `(required)` beside its
  label stands in amber on its ground, in bold, and the field has a mark down its left
  edge while it is still empty (P-260).

## Changed

- **A code that a region has is refused for a site, and a site's for a region**,
  naming the record that has it, so that a path that begins with a code leads to one
  place. A region and a site that hold one code from before stay as they are until
  the code of one is changed (P-237).
- **The username in the list of contacts, and who made a change in the audit trail and
  in the history of an item, open the user's page for an Admin** (P-239).
- **The rows of the summary and of the management report by vendor, by owner, by site
  and by region open that record's page**, and the record of an audit event that was
  deleted is text that says so (P-240).
- **A model's kind is one of six.** `virtual_context` and `container` join `hardware`,
  `virtual_machine`, `software` and `service`, and both are among the kinds counted
  from the start. An installation that has set `count_kinds` itself keeps what it set.
- **Like columns stand in one sequence on every list.** The columns of a place stand
  from the smallest outward, Location, Site, Region, then Planned location; after them
  Operator, then Owner; and Tags before Record status. So the item list, the items of a
  vendor and the report of the end of life show Location before Site, where Site stood
  first, and the device groups have their places before their operator and owner. **A
  layout that a browser has kept for a list is left as it is**: a person who has sorted,
  filtered or moved anything on a list keeps the old order there until they press Reset
  layout and filters (P-253).
- **A replacement changes of each item what its request names, and nothing by a rule**,
  where it changed nothing of either: a replacement dated ahead changes nothing before
  its day, and is refused with anything that would.
- **An unchanged file of a tool writes one entry in the audit trail, and not one for
  each thing.** A file read from a system that only confirms what is there moves the
  time each registration was last confirmed and writes no event for it; the import's own
  event says how many it confirmed.
- **What an operator has from an item** counts what runs on one as well as what is
  installed in one, on the scoreboard and on the operator's page.

## Fixes

- **A file chosen for a place before the page had read the kinds of file was given no
  kind**, and the person chose it: a picture is a photograph now when the kinds
  arrive, and a kind the person chose is never changed. Older than 0.9.7, where it
  failed one scenario once on a busy host.

## Additional Notes

- Upgrading from 0.9.7 runs two migrations, and the schema goes from revision `0012`
  to `0014`. **Neither changes a record that is held, and neither writes an audit
  event.** `0013` lets an item be replaced more than once; `0014` adds to an item what
  it runs on, and to an import the system it was read from. See "From 0.9.7 to 0.9.8"
  in [Install and operate](../install.md#from-097-to-098).
- **After upgrading.** Virtual machines that were entered before 0.9.8 are marked as
  naming no host, and the first page counts them: name their hosts. One whose model
  was entered as hardware has its model's kind set first; one that was entered as
  installed in its server is made to run on it in one press on its page, or by a file
  with `host` and `parent` `!clear`. An installation that has set `count_kinds` itself
  adds `virtual_context` and `container` to it where it wants them counted. A list
  whose layout a browser has kept shows the new order of its columns after Reset
  layout and filters.
- Going back to 0.9.7 is by the backup that the upgrade made: its schema cannot hold an
  item that was replaced twice, nor what runs on what.
- **The API gains**: `drop_identity`, `disposition`, `expected_online`, `old_item` and
  `hand_over_hosted` on `POST /items/{hid}/replace`; `condition` in what an item is
  given when it leaves; `replacements` on an item; `POST /items/{hid}/make-stack`;
  `POST /item-replacements/{hid}/remove`; on an item `host_item_hid`, `host_group_hid`,
  `host`, `observed_host_hid`, `observed_host`, `host_observed_at`, `host_differs`,
  `hosted_count`, `review` and `effective.serial_number`; `GET /items/{hid}/hosted` and
  `GET /device-groups/{hid}/hosted`; `dry_run` and `hosted` on `POST /items/retire`,
  and `depends_on` in the refusal of a group that something runs on; `system`,
  `complete`, `not_reported`, `not_reported_held` and `not_reported_first` on an
  import, and the column `external_id` of the items file; the status `not_reported` of
  a registration; `source_label` on the owner in force and `reached_by` on a step of
  the walk; and the kinds `virtual_context` and `container`.
- **The API changes** in three places. An item is replaced, and takes a place, more
  than once: `replaces` and `replaced_by` are the latest of each. The source of a value
  may be `host`. And `from_item`, of what an operator has, counts what runs on an item
  as well as what is installed in one.
- **What the walk through hosts costs.** An inventory in which nothing runs on
  anything pays nothing: the step is left out of every statement. Measured on a quiet
  build host, on 50,000 fictional items of which 2,000 run on a host, against the same
  inventory with none, the least of seven runs: the first page of the item list 134 to
  145 ms against 139 to 159; the list sorted by location 208 to 217 ms against 189 to
  217; filtered to one site 246 to 257 ms against 208 to 232; the summary 2.0 s against
  1.5 s; the geography tree 0.70 s against 0.55 s. One gain was taken; the rest is
  work on the shape of the walk, which goes to the timing of 1.0.0.
- P-237 to P-253 and P-260 are this release's, each in its row of
  [Decisions](../decisions.md), with what the owner said. Five reviewers who had not
  built the work read it, three the whole of it and two what was mended and decided
  after: none found a fault in what a deletion, a retirement or a
  replacement does to what runs on a host, in some seventy trials of two requests at
  one moment among them. What they found was mended before the release. Among it: a
  replacement dated ahead took the name and the place from a spare months before it
  joined; a row of a tool's file that gave both an identifier of `hirdir` and the
  tool's own was held to neither, and could give two machines one identifier; a
  machine that is retired, still reported by its tool, was quietly renamed; a tool's
  file that came out empty would have marked every machine; the page of what an
  operator is on no longer added up once a machine had a host; a planned place that
  was opened left a machine with a place of its own behind; a choice of what becomes
  of a member wiped what had been typed; and, in the last reading, a second virtual
  context of one chassis that was reported before its switch was refused as a
  duplicate, which was the owner's own case.
- **Decided by the owner while this release was reviewed**: a row of a tool's file
  whose identifier finds an item that is retired is rejected; a tool's unchanged file
  writes one entry in the audit trail; the serial number given for a virtual context
  names its host (P-251); what runs on a stack runs on the stack (P-252); like columns
  in one sequence, the places from the smallest outward, and a kept layout left as it
  is (P-253); and a field that must be filled marked at a glance (P-260).
- **On the roadmap, after 1.0.0**, at the owner's word (P-254 to P-259): a report of
  discrepancies, the gaps that should be filled, each with a link to where its fix is
  begun; mail by SMTP through a smart host, for notices and for reports on a schedule;
  a mailed report as its detail, a link, or both; a message of the day; a maintenance
  mode; and each connector paused and resumed by itself.
- Left for a later release, each seen: a module that is installed in a switch of a stack
  may be named as a host by itself, and stays one; a membership that begins on a day to
  come counts from the day it is entered, so a replacement of a stack's member entered
  ahead of its day hands what runs on the item that comes to the stack at once, which is
  older than this release; a model changed from a virtual context to another kind while
  contexts of it wait for their switch leaves them the number they hold, which then
  finds nothing; a virtual context that has a host and is reported with a serial number
  that no item has keeps its host, and nothing says so; the capacity lines have their
  vendor before their places, which the sequence of columns does not name; the bulk
  edit's preview names a host by its serial number, the key a file has for an item; with
  items that run on a host in it, the summary of a large inventory takes about a third
  longer, which goes to the timing of 1.0.0; and everything in a browser was tried in
  Chromium, not in Edge or Firefox. From 0.9.7 there remain the box in a list's heading
  that ticks the rows read so far, a hundred at a time, and a fault outside drawing that
  is written to the console and shown nowhere.
- **Under several people at once.** The server is one process and works on one
  processor. With items that run on a host in an inventory, every statement that reads
  a value in force is larger, and each request costs that processor more. Alone on the
  build host each request is quick, the scoreboard's three tables 0.3 to 0.4 s each on
  an inventory of 1,383 items of which 39 are hosted; with four browsers driving the
  whole of the scenarios at once, the server fell behind until the scoreboard was
  still counting after 20 to 30 seconds, and three scenarios that wait for it failed,
  which pass by themselves; with three at once all pass, in the same time, so the
  release was checked three at once. A handful of people at their daily work are far
  from that; several at once on the scoreboard and the reports of a large inventory
  with hosted items may wait for their counts. It goes with the cost of the walk to
  the timing of 1.0.0.
- Twenty-seven browser scenarios are new, in five files, and seven files of earlier
  releases were changed for what changed on purpose: 225 in all. They add no numbered
  scenario, as a release of corrections adds none.
- The package test upgrades an installation of 0.9.7 through both migrations, with
  what each could harm made beforehand and looked at after; makes the second migration
  fail, to see the upgrade stop, undo both and go back; and installs on a system that
  never had `hirdir`. It carries no `SKIP`.
- Every component is at its latest production release. Nothing is held back.
- Scenarios S01 to S35 and S39 to S53 pass.
