# hirdir 0.9.10

The server says how it is doing, and answers at once to wait where it would have let a
request time out; and the imports and bulk edits are applied by a second process, so
that the answers to people are not slowed by them.

## New

- **A mark of the server's health in the menu bar** (P-270, P-272). Every page shows,
  beside the name of the person signed in, how quick the server's answers are: a green
  circle while the slowest of the last half minute took under a second, a yellow
  triangle from one second, an orange diamond from two, a red square past three. Each
  has its words as its name and under the pointer, for whoever cannot tell the colours
  apart, and it leads to About. It asks the server nothing: every answer of the API
  carries the health in a header, `X-Hirdir-Health`, and the mark shows what the newest
  answer said, the answer that was slow among them. On a quiet server one slow answer
  changes the colour for half a minute, which is what it is for: to see poor
  performance while it happens, and not only under a crowd.
- **About shows the server's last ten minutes** (P-266, P-273), to everyone signed in,
  read again every five seconds while the page is in front and not otherwise: the
  health in words, with the measure it stands on and what is refused; the time of an
  answer at its lowest, average and highest and at the 50th, 95th and 99th in a
  hundred; how many requests fell under one second, in one to two, in two to three and
  over three; how many were answered well, not taken as asked, failed, and refused as
  busy; the requests in flight; the share of a processor the server used; the memory of
  the process and of the host, the space free where the inventory is kept, the
  processors and the load of the host; whether the second process runs; and a graph of
  the ten minutes, the time of an answer above and the processor below on one axis of
  time, with a mark at every step in which an answer of busy was given. The page draws
  the graph itself, with no library.
- **What was slow, and for whom.** About lists the ten slowest requests of the ten
  minutes, each by its route, never by what was asked for or searched, with the time
  it took, its status, who asked, and its request ID, by which it is found in the
  server's journal; the slowest reports, exports and previews apart, being long by
  nature. And it lists who was active, with how many of each person's requests fell in
  each band of time, the slowest, how many were refused as busy and how many failed,
  whoever had it worst first, so that the team that looks after the installation sees
  who is having a bad time of it. A service token is listed under its account, as such;
  requests that say nothing of who asks, a sign-in among them, are one row together.
- **Past a ceiling the server answers at once to wait** (P-262, P-271, P-274). Nothing
  is refused at green and yellow. At orange the reports, the exports and the previews
  are answered `503` Busy at once, with `Retry-After`, and at red every read is, the
  lists among them; the page says that the server is busy, with the seconds to wait and
  Try again, where a request would have waited in a queue and timed out. The page
  itself, a sign-in, every change that is stored, About and the settings are never
  refused. What is refused is decided by how long the reads alone take, and only with
  more than twenty of them in the half minute: a few slow answers on a quiet server are
  no load to shed, and changes waiting their turn behind a long import turn no read
  away. The processor makes the server orange, never red. After red the server is
  orange for a further half minute.
- **Three settings, under Admin, Settings**: `busy_response_ms`, 3000 unless set, whose
  thirds are the four colours; `busy_cpu_percent`, 95 unless set, the share of one
  processor past which the server is orange, which can pass 100 on a host of several
  processors and takes up to 100 for each; and `busy_over_seconds`, 30 unless set, the
  time the measures are taken over. They hold from the moment they are saved. A measure
  set to 0 is not looked at, and both at 0 refuse nothing.
- **A second process applies the imports and bulk edits** (P-263, P-267, P-269):
  `hirdir admin work`, the service `hirdir-worker` beside `hirdir`, started, stopped
  and restarted with it and under the same limits. It answers nothing on the network,
  looks for work every second, and records the capacity balance of the week. The
  server no longer applies anything, so a long import takes a processor of its own
  while people are answered. `journalctl -u hirdir-worker` shows what it did.

## Changed

- **An apply waits to be taken up.** `POST /imports/{hid}/apply`, and the apply of a
  bulk edit, answer with the job `queued`, always; the second process makes it
  `applying` within a second. One asked for while that process is not running waits,
  and is applied when it starts. The pages say "Waiting to be applied".
- **The commands that need the service stopped need both processes stopped.**
  `hirdir admin restore`, `reset` and `load-examples` refuse while either runs, and say
  which; `restore` did not refuse before. `systemctl stop hirdir` stops both.
- **`hirdir admin serve --work`** runs both in one process, as every release before
  did; `make dev` uses it.
- **A busy answer waits before it asks again.** A page that follows an import, and the
  list of past imports, wait as long as the server said before they read again.

## Additional Notes

- Upgrading from 0.9.9 changes nothing that is held: there is no migration, and the
  schema stays at revision `0014`. The upgrade installs and starts `hirdir-worker`.
  See "From 0.9.9 to 0.9.10" in [Install and operate](../install.md#from-099-to-0910).
- Going back to 0.9.9 needs no backup: it is the one `apt install --allow-downgrades`
  command, which takes the second unit away.
- **The ceiling is on from the upgrade**, at three seconds, 95% and thirty seconds.
  What watches the server through a proxy will see `503` with `Retry-After` under load
  where it saw requests time out.
- **The long reports of the day stay with the server** in this release (P-269): the
  coverage report, the renewal worklist, the summary of the renewal alert and the
  capacity balance are worked out by the server at its start and at five minutes past
  midnight, since what is worked out is kept in its memory. They move to the second
  process in the release after, as the owner decided.
- **The lists are not put into pages** when the server is slow (P-271): a list already
  reads a hundred rows at a time as it is scrolled. What a list costs at each read is
  the count of everything that matches, which is a note for the timing of 1.0.0.
- **The measures are kept nowhere.** They are in the memory of the server, ten minutes
  of them, and start empty whenever it starts. Nothing of them is written to the
  database or to disk.
- **The API gains**, with nothing taken away: `GET /about/load`; the header
  `X-Hirdir-Health` on every answer; the member `busy` in the problem of a `503` that
  the ceiling gave, which the `503` of the one writer has not; and the three settings.
  The answer to an apply says `queued` where it could say `applying`.
- Two independent reviews read the server's side while it was built. The first found
  that a stall that was over kept the server busy, that the processor's share passes
  100, since the database works in several threads at once, and that requests whose
  body never came held the server busy for good. The second found that changes waiting
  behind a long import turned every read away, and that the answer that was slow
  itself said that all was well. All are mended and held by tests.
- Two things are known and left: when the service starts in a week that has no record
  of the capacity balance yet, the long reports worked out at that start are worked
  out once more for the first person who asks; and a server that fails and is started
  again by systemd takes the second process with it, so an import that was being
  applied is `stopped` and waits to be resumed, as it did in 0.9.9.
- The browser scenarios run with no ceiling, but for one file that sets one and
  drives the server past it; the tests of the routes likewise.
- **One browser scenario failed once and was not reproduced.** In one whole run of the
  scenarios on the build host, the scenario of P-202, what is typed into an item's
  details after Save, found Save unavailable at its third save and waited out its two
  minutes. It passed in the next whole run, by itself, with the answer to the save held
  back three seconds, and with the page's reading of the item held back. The scenario
  goes on from the words "The item was saved", which may be those of the save before,
  so it runs ahead of the answer to its second save; no fault of the page was found,
  and none is ruled out. If Save is ever found unavailable on an item's details with
  something typed, that is the thing to report.
- Every component is at its latest production release. Nothing is held back.
- Scenarios S01 to S35 and S39 to S53 pass. The ceiling and the second process have no
  scenario of the requirements yet: they are held by the tests of the server, by the
  browser scenarios and by the package test.
