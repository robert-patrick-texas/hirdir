# hirdir 0.9.6

A third review of the web UI, in three parts: what the first two reviews had measured
and left; the pages they had not walked, purchases and receiving, a vendor's page,
contracts, renewals and agreements, with an improvement to each; and working a stack of
switches, for an organization with hundreds of stacks and a fair bit of churn in them
each week. Each change was measured in a browser before and after, on the fictional
examples, on a fictional inventory of some 47,000 items, and on one of 386 stacks and
1,843 switches. Nothing that is held changes: there is no migration. The API gains a
route and several members, listed under Additional Notes.

## New

- **A switch joins a stack in one step.** Add members on a stack's page has a box,
  ticked from the start, It takes the stack's name and place: the switch's own host
  name, FQDN and addresses are removed and its own location cleared, so that the
  stack's is in force, and a spare becomes Production, all in the one change with the
  membership. The form says exactly what will be removed from the switch before it is
  sent; unticked, the switch keeps its own. Nothing of the stack is copied to the
  member. Adding a spare took 4 clicks, then another page, 8 clicks and 3 saves to put
  its name, place and standing right; it takes 3 clicks and 12 keys. The file of
  members has the same as columns.
- **A member that leaves is given what becomes of it.** Ending a membership of a stack
  asks What becomes of it: to the spare pool, with a new name and a place; to be
  returned to the manufacturer; excess; or nothing now. A person chooses, the fields
  it fills can be changed, and the server checks each by the item's own rules, in the
  one change with the end. The row that ended stays in view. Sending a member back to
  the pool took 14 clicks; it takes 4. Replacing a failed member on the stack's page,
  End as returned and then Add, which offers the position just freed, took some 21
  clicks over 3 pages and takes 6 on one.
- **A stack is found by its name, wherever an item is looked for.** Find an item and
  the item list look in the names of the groups an item is a member of, and say above
  the rows that the words name a stack, with a link to it and how many members it has;
  typing a stack's name answered "No items". The list of groups finds a stack by a
  member's serial number. The item list has a column, Stack, beside Name: the stack and
  the member's position, for switches that have no name of their own.
- **A member's page says it is a member.** Among its first facts: Member, its position,
  of its stack, as a link, with the stack's host name, FQDN and addresses, each said to
  be the stack's and read from it. A switch in no stack has Join a stack there, which
  finds the stack by its name: from a spare's own page nothing led to a stack before.
- **The picker of members says where each switch stands.** Each switch offered shows
  its state and disposition, where it is, and the stack it is in with its position;
  the spares come first; one that is in a stack cannot be chosen for another, and says
  why, where it was refused only after Add.
- **A catalog entry is chosen by searching.** In New item, an item's details, a
  successor, a bulk edit, a replacement and a purchase line, the catalog entry is a box
  that searches as it is typed in, each word in the PID, the model name and the
  vendor's name, the most used first. It was a list of every entry, 319 on the large
  inventory, that typing did not search.
- **A model that is not in the catalog is added from New item.** Under the search, Add
  this model to the catalog: its vendor, PID, model name, kind and type, within the
  page. The entry is made, chosen, and the keyboard goes on to the next field; what was
  typed stays. Where the catalog has the PID already, the entry that has it is offered,
  Use it. It took 13 clicks over 4 pages and lost what had been typed; it takes 3
  clicks on one.
- **A list keeps its place.** Coming back to a list from a record, by the browser's
  Back or by a link, shows the rows it was on, scrolled as it was, with the row that
  was opened marked. A new filter or sort begins at the top. The place is kept for the
  tab, in the browser's session storage.
- **A contact's page lists where the contact is named.** Every record that names the
  contact, by kind: places, owners, operators, agreements, contracts, vendors and
  circuits, each a link with the role the contact has there, from
  `GET /contacts/{hid}/uses`. On the large inventory the pages of 75 contacts listed
  10 of the 153 records that name them, and list them all.
- **A count of a report opens the items it counts.** Every count of items, components
  and health in the summary and the management report is a link to the item list, by a
  filter the server gives with the count. On the management report by site none of 604
  numbers could be opened; 241 can, the rest being money, managed nodes and weights,
  which are not lists of items.
- **A refusal says where to go.** A record refused because another has its serial
  number, asset tag, PID, name, label or number names that record as a link. A filter
  or a sort of a field its list does not have names the nearest fields it has: `type`
  on the item list is answered with `catalog.type`.
- **Receiving opens ready to type, where the line is going.** Receive on a purchase
  line opens its form in the window with the keyboard in the box of what arrived, and
  its location is looked for at the line's destination site, the site's one location
  chosen where it has only one. The form began 631 pixels down a window of 800 with the
  location at Not said.
- **A request's page.** Add and add another like it keeps the destination site and the
  expected date of the last line; one link, Every item it brought, opens the items that
  arrived on its lines; and the table of lines fits the window, where its buttons were
  cut off.
- **What is still to come for a site.** The plain words of the list of purchase lines
  look in the destination site's name, and Destination site and Expected stand within
  the window. A site's name found nothing before, and the answer took 39 keys.
- **A vendor's page at size.** The table of models shows its first twenty and Show
  all; the vendor's items stand above its agreements and contracts, with the filter box
  of the item list, which narrows within the vendor's items. The page of the largest
  vendor was 30.9 windows tall and is 13.2, and which contracts cover its items that
  lose support within a year can be answered there.
- **Contracts, renewals and agreements.** On Contracts the grid of every contract comes
  first, with the tables by agreement after it, folded: it began 17,120 pixels down.
  On Renewals the rows of a count that is opened come into the window. The items a
  contract covers have the filter box. The list of agreements shows the account
  manager, and an agreement's page what each of its contracts costs a year and their
  total, leaving out those cancelled or expired.
- **The renewal form starts from what follows.** Renew begins with the year after the
  present term and a blank purchase reference, with Keep the present period for a new
  number alone, and says under its button how many coverages follow, how many items
  are marked not to be renewed, and how many begin with the new term.
- **Leave out of the renewal.** On the items a contract covers, the rows selected have
  Leave out of the renewal, which sets Not to be renewed on those items, and Renew
  after all, which takes it off. The mark is the item's own and holds for every
  contract that covers it, which the page says first. It meant opening each item and a
  box 6,024 pixels down its page.
- **A minor item's page says what it is and where.** Under its heading: its type and
  model, its position in the item it is in, as a link, and its site; and a link, Spares
  of this model here, to the list of them. The page of a power supply was headed by
  its serial number alone.
- **A page that has made a record says so**, with a link to it: a region, a site, a
  location, a catalog entry and a contact did not.

## Changed

- **The walks of why a record has each value are folded.** On the page of an item and
  of a device group they stand behind a line, Why it has each value, closed when the
  page opens and read when it is opened. They took 2.2 windows above the form.
- **A position in a stack is held by one member.** Adding a member at a position that
  another current member holds, or moving one there, is refused, naming the member
  that holds it. The position offered is the lowest that is free, and a member added
  with none takes it, by the API and by a file. A file of members is judged when all
  of its rows are in, so that it still swaps two members or renumbers a stack; by the
  API two members change places by way of a free position. Stacks that hold two at one
  position already stay as they are until one is changed.
- **The item list has the column Stack from the start**, beside Name, and the columns
  of 0.9.5 are narrowed to stand within 1280 pixels with it. A layout kept before stays
  as it was until Reset layout and filters.
- **The export of the item list holds the host name** with the name.
- **A grid on one record's page remembers no filter.** A vendor's items and the items
  a contract covers keep their columns, widths and sort, and no filter, so that what is
  typed on one record's page is not in force on the next.
- **The list of purchase lines** has Part number and Tracked in the column chooser, to
  bring Destination site and Expected into the window.

## Fixes

- **What is typed after a save is kept.** A box filled in the moment after Save, while
  the page read the record again, was filled over. It keeps what was typed, and the
  next save is not refused as stale. Where another person changed the same field
  meanwhile, the form says beneath it what the record now has and that saving replaces
  it.
- **An end that was not kept is not announced.** Ending a coverage on its contract's
  own last day changes nothing, since such an item follows a renewal, and the page said
  "3 coverages were ended". It says before anything is sent that this changes nothing
  and how to leave an item out, and after an End it says what the server kept.
- **The renewal form showed a purchase reference that it did not send.**
- **A list a form reads whole stopped at 1,000 records.** The 1,001st contact could
  not be chosen. Such a list is read to its end.
- **A line's destination site could not be a draft site**, though a line of the
  examples names one. Draft sites are offered, marked.
- **The preview of a bulk edit** spoke of the order of the file and offered rows to
  create.
- **A report's link to its items left out the view's switches**, and listed fewer
  items than the total beside it with archived items on. The total is the link.
- **A refusal that began with a host name** was shown with the name capitalised.

## Additional Notes

- Upgrading from 0.9.5 changes nothing that is held: there is no migration, and the
  schema stays at revision `0012`. See "From 0.9.5 to 0.9.6" in
  [Install and operate](../install.md#from-095-to-096).
- Going back to 0.9.5 needs no backup: it is the one `apt install --allow-downgrades`
  command.
- **The API gains**, with nothing taken away: `GET /contacts/{hid}/uses`; `filter` on
  the totals and rows of `GET /reports/summary` and `GET /reports/management`, with
  `conditions`; `existing` in the problem document of a duplicate; `drop_identity`,
  `disposition` and `expected_online` on a member added to a device group, `item` on a
  membership ended, and `next_position` on a device group. An audit event of a change
  made by joining or leaving a group says so, with the group, in its changes.
- **What a change removes is kept in the audit trail and nowhere else** (P-223). The
  host name, the FQDN and each address a switch drops on joining a stack are audit
  events, with the switch and the stack. Working them into an IPAM, a DNS and
  monitoring tools, in batches read from the audit trail, comes after 1.0.0.
- **A purchase line has no price** (P-225): prices stay in the procurement system a
  request's reference points to.
- P-198 to P-225 are this release's, each in its row of [Decisions](../decisions.md)
  with what was measured. Four reviewers who had not built the work read it; what they
  found was mended before the release, among it a link under the renewal form that
  left the page and lost what was typed, a filter typed on one vendor's page that was
  in force on every other, a field kept after a save that silently replaced what
  another person had saved, and a location sent as chosen that the form showed as a
  bare identifier.
- **`make browser`** runs the browser scenarios on the build host against a fresh
  scratch inventory, runs each project that runs alone whatever failed before it, and
  ends with one list of every scenario that failed; `--last-failed` runs only those
  again, and `--since <commit>` only the files that changed. The scenarios of places
  are three files, where one file of 4.0 minutes set the least the suite could take.
- Left for a later release, each seen and measured: one press, Replace, on a member's
  row, which needs an item to be replaced, and to take another's place, more than once
  (O-02); a stack made of a switch that stands alone, from its page; the box of a
  joining member in New group; the condition of a member that leaves, set with the
  end; a count of components or of health opens the item list with its filter written
  as a tree, which is not narrowed by typing; a grid drops its selection when its
  filter changes, so that three named items of a long list are found one at a time;
  the group a device group is in, the site of a capacity line, the contacts of an
  owner and of a place and the agreement of a contract are still lists that typing
  does not search; a coverage that begins with a contract's next term is shown as
  ending with the present one; the list of requests does not show where each is going
  or when it is due, nor the list of vendors how many items each has; a location is
  not found by the name of a region above its site's own; the menu bar takes three
  rows at 1280 pixels; and the reports on an inventory of some 47,000 items take 2 to
  9 seconds, which goes to the timing of 1.0.0.
- `npm audit` reports an advisory against `picomatch`, which Vite and Vitest use when
  the web UI is built and tested. No release of them is without it yet; it is not in
  the package, which holds the built files alone.
- Thirty-one browser scenarios are new, in five files, one for each lane of the review,
  and one of 0.9.5 went with the sentence it tested: 163 in all. They add no numbered
  scenario, as a release of corrections adds none.
- The package test carries one `SKIP`: with no migration there is none to make fail, so
  that check is skipped and says so. The pass upgrades and goes back all the same.
- Every component is at its latest production release. Nothing is held back.
- Scenarios S01 to S35 and S39 to S53 pass.
