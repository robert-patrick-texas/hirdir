# hirdir 0.9.19

The round of speed, before 1.0.0. An import of 10,000 rows previews in under 30 seconds
and applies in under a minute, where it took three minutes each; a large preview is
worked in the background, as an apply is, and the page follows it; and the item list at
50,000 items answers its filtered, sorted pages within its target.

## New

- **A large preview is worked in the background** (P-325, migration `0020`). A file of
  more than a hundred rows, sent on the Import page, by `POST /imports/preview` or by
  `hirdir import`, and a bulk edit of more than a hundred items, are answered `202` with
  the job, `previewing`; the second process works it out and the job is then
  `previewed`, as one previewed at once is, and applied as any preview is. The page says
  Waiting to be previewed, in its turn, then Previewing 3,200 of 10,000 rows, may be left
  and come back to, and says why a preview failed, with Preview again. A file of a
  hundred rows or fewer is previewed at once, as before, so that a small file is not held
  up behind a large apply, as the owner chose. `GET /imports/{hid}` says how far it has
  got in `rows_previewed`; while it is `previewing` its rows and its apply answer `409`.
  The CLI waits for it and says how far it has got, and says once where the background
  work is not running.

## Changed

- **An import is some five times quicker** (P-143, P-324). On the fixture of 50,000 items
  and an idle host, 10,000 new items preview in 27.2 s, against 169.8 s, and apply in
  36.4 s, against 183.4 s. Each row is worked as before and answers the same; what it
  costs is less: the copy a preview is worked on is one transaction, the records of a
  batch are read back together under a rule that keeps each row's answer as it stood
  after its own write, what a file of items cannot change is looked up once for the file
  or the batch, what a batch names is read ahead for it, and a new item and its audit
  event are stored together.
- **A sign-in waits less behind an apply** (P-324). Between two batches the apply lets
  the one writer's place go only while another change is under way, and waits for it a
  second at most, where it paused 0.12 s after every batch: the pauses of a 10,000-row
  apply took 0.4 s, against 23.9 s, and a sign-in during it waited 131 ms beyond its
  idle time at the median and 342 ms at the longest, against 387 ms and 1.2 s. Every
  process that writes holds `writing.lock` in the state directory while it does.
- **The item list answers sooner** (S36, P-326). On the fixture and an idle host every page of the item list that the timing asks for, 25 of them, answers within its target, 300 ms at the median and 600 ms at the 95th percentile, where 15 did not at the start of 0.9.19: the slowest, the items with no cover sorted by when their cover ends, in 278 / 321 ms, against 534 / 806 ms, and the grid's first page in 150 / 174 ms. What is in force for each contract on the day is worked out once in a page's one statement, which also counts it; a page is shaped with what it reads held for it; and the server keeps what it is made with out of the later passes of Python's collector, which made the slowest of fifteen twice the median. Every page answers as it did.
- **The coverage report worked out anew takes 0.8 s**, against 1.4 s, by the same
  reading of what is in force for each contract once.
- **Every count of the Import page and of the bulk edit** is written with its thousands'
  separator, and the page says what changes once, by state and by quarter, where it said
  how far an apply had got at every reading.

## Fixes

- A preview of more than a minute failed behind nginx, which gives up on a request
  after 60 seconds, while the server went on and stored it; it is now worked in the
  background (P-325).
- `make time-filters` drove the server past its own ceiling, which then refused every
  long report it was asked to time; it times with the ceiling off (P-327).
- A circuit's links to one device came in no set order on a device's page.

## Additional Notes

- **Upgrade.** Migration `0020` adds `rows_previewed` to every import job, equal to its
  row count, and changes no record. A script that sends a preview of more than a hundred
  rows is answered `202` where it was answered `201`, and follows the job with
  `GET /imports/{hid}`. The second unit, `hirdir-worker`, must be running for a large
  preview to be worked, as it must for an apply. See
  [From 0.9.18 to 0.9.19](../install.md#from-0918-to-0919).
- **The owner's answers** (P-325, P-324, P-326): a long preview in the background; a
  small file previewed at once; the bulk edit as imports are; one more step for the
  preview when it stood at 30.8 s; and the shaping of a page made cheaper when two pages
  of the list were just over.
- **This release's test** (P-305, an ordinary release): `make check`, `make acceptance`,
  the browser scenarios on the host of every spec file changed since 0.9.18, the timing
  alone on the host, one `make deb`, and of the package test `--upgrade` from 0.9.18,
  `--failed-upgrade`, `--fresh` and `--browser-check`.
- **Reviews.** Two of the server and two of the web in a browser. They found, and the
  release mends: a bulk edit of tags previewed in the background that undid a tag set
  while it waited; how far a preview had got leaving out the rows that could not be
  read; a command that waited without a word for background work that was not running;
  the page re-announcing a failure as a new preview began, a preview waiting its turn
  that looked stalled, and progress read aloud at every reading; and the background
  work keeping a job under way at its start from Python's collector for good.
- **Left as they are**, for 1.0.0's accessibility pass and after: the focus lost to the
  page after Apply, Resume, the bulk edit's Close and a refused preview; a server that
  cannot be reached said only by the list of past imports, again every two seconds; a
  preview in the background that shows Waiting to be previewed for a moment though
  nothing is ahead of it; on PostgreSQL, which is not yet run, a preview in the
  background holds the one writer for its whole length. The copy a large preview is
  worked on is made in the private temporary directory of `hirdir-worker`, the size of
  the database.
- **Next, 1.0.0**: the accessibility pass, the user and operator documentation completed,
  the backup timer and restore checked, the upgrade from the release just before alone,
  and this release's timing run again on the release, alone on the host.
