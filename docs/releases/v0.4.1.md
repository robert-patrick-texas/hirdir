# hirdir 0.4.1

The answers to the questions that 0.4.0 left open, and what they brought with them:
how an item is doing, as a condition, an observation and a health; a stack whose master
is what the stack makes it; and a cluster that says how it appears on the network.

## New

- **Condition.** An item has a condition, the state of its hardware as a person knows
  it: untested, good, degraded, suspect, failed or dead. The list can be edited. It
  holds while the item is on a shelf, and it moves the item nowhere in the lifecycle.
- **What is observed.** An item, a stack and a cluster have an observed status, with
  the moment it was seen: online, degraded or unreachable on the network, or present,
  which is what a person saw. Present is for what is never on the network, a fibre
  tester or a power unit with no management connection, and for the spare that was
  counted at the last audit. It is set by hand or loaded from a file, until a
  monitoring tool writes it, which comes after 1.0.0.
- **Health.** From the condition, what is observed and whether the item is meant to be
  online, each item has a health: good, degraded, failed or not known, shown as a mark
  in green, amber, red or grey, with the reasons. A stack and a cluster have one too,
  from their members: a stack with a failed member, with a member in Down or
  Pending-Removal, or with fewer members than it is meant to have, is degraded. It is
  worked out when it is read, so it is right the moment anything changes.
- **The master that is seen.** Which switch of a stack is master is decided by the
  stack as it boots, and may move. A stack now records the master that is seen, with
  the moment, apart from the one that is intended, and shows the serial number of the
  one seen. Where the two differ the stack says so, and that is no fault. A stack is
  found by the serial number of any of its members.
- **How a cluster is managed.** A cluster says whether it is one device on the
  network, as a pair of wireless LAN controllers is, or whether each member is managed
  at its own address, as a pair of firewalls is, or that and a node of its own as well.
  It is counted so in the managed-node count.
- **A management report.** `GET /reports/management`, `hirdir report management` and a
  tab under Reports: the estimated value and the annual support cost of the inventory,
  whole and by vendor, kind, type, function, owner or tag. By site follows with sites,
  in 0.5.0.
- **A management address of each family.** A device may be managed over IPv4, over
  IPv6, over both or over neither, and has a primary address of each. In a file,
  `mgmt_ipv4` and `mgmt_ipv6` set them and `mgmt_address` takes either.
- **A first file loads as it is.** A file of host names, with or without addresses,
  makes the items, and loaded again changes nothing. The column names such a file is
  likely to use, `ip_address`, `ip`, `ipv4_address`, `ipv4`, `ipv6_address` and `ipv6`,
  are read as the columns they mean.
- **A site's own file finds the items it has.** A row with neither serial number nor
  asset tag finds the one item that has that name or that host name, whether or not
  the item has a serial number. So a file of host names with the status another tool
  reports, or with addresses, changes the switches that are there and makes no second
  item beside one. More than one item answering is an error on the row, never a guess.

## Changed

- **A stack needs no master and may be one switch alone.** In 0.4.0 a stack had to have
  two members and one master, and the master could not leave. A stack waits for its
  second switch in staging, and is left with one when a member fails. The role
  `master` now marks the master that is intended: optional, one at most, and nothing
  stops a member leaving.
- **The summary holds no money.** `GET /reports/summary` and `hirdir report summary` no
  longer give estimated value and annual support cost: those are in the management
  report. The summary gives the components by health and by condition instead, and can
  be by condition and by health.
- **One switch alone earns no stack discount.** The discount on support weight is for a
  stack of two or more.
- **Leaving the team's hands ends memberships.** Retiring an item, or moving it to
  Released or Archived, ends its memberships of groups that day. A move to any other
  state leaves them, so a failed switch in Pending-Removal is still a member of its
  stack, and the stack shows as degraded. An item that is Released or Archived joins
  no group, and takes no member's place in a replacement.
- **A replacement hands over the role, not what was seen.** The item that takes a
  member's place is the intended master if the old one was. Nobody has seen it as
  master, so the stack has no master that is seen until one is recorded.
- **Stating again what is seen is looking again.** A person who sets the observed
  status to what it already is has checked again, and the moment becomes now. A file
  that states it again with no moment changes nothing, so the same file loaded twice
  changes nothing.
- **The item grid leaves minor items out** until "Include minor items" is turned on.
  The switch is remembered for each person, and what is copied, exported or edited in
  bulk is what the grid shows.
- **Controllers are found through nested groups.** A controller domain may hold several
  clusters, an east pair and a west pair, at any depth. An access point that is an
  endpoint of the domain lists the controllers of all of them, each with its cluster.
- **A shared address belongs to the group.** The examples now hold the virtual address
  that two routers share as an address of their cluster, once, so it is no duplicate,
  and the duplicates report is left with the repeats nobody meant.

## Additional Notes

- Every component is at its latest production release. Nothing is held back.
- Upgrading from 0.4.0 takes the schema from revision `0005` to `0006`: the items and
  the device groups gain columns, and no table is created. Every cluster is counted as
  one managed node, as before, until it is told otherwise, and no item has a condition
  until somebody gives it one. One thing that is held is changed: the memberships of
  items that are Released or Archived, which 0.4.0 left standing, are ended on the day
  of the upgrade, each with an entry in the audit log. See "From 0.4.0 to 0.4.1" in
  [Install and operate](../install.md#upgrade).
- For anything that reads the API: the totals and rows of `GET /reports/summary` no
  longer hold `est_value` and `annual_support_cost`, and `by_kind` holds `key` and
  `label` where it held `kind`. A stack's `serial_number` may now be blank, and
  `serial_source` says which master it is read from.
- A condition that a site adds to the list says nothing about health either way: only
  the six that ship are known to it.
- Untested is not known good, and shows grey. Unreachable is red only for what is
  expected online; for anything else it is amber.
- The health of a stack does not turn amber because its master moved.
- Sorting by health puts the worst first. Addresses filter, sort and export by family.
- A moment that is observed is not ahead of now by more than a day, is read as UTC when
  it names no zone, and is not cleared by itself.
- An independent review of the server before release found eight defects. All are
  corrected, each with a test.
- The Python tests and the scenarios take a sixth of the time they took, and the
  three passes of the package test run side by side. They run as many at once as the
  build host has free, and wait for a busy host for ten minutes at the most. See "How
  long the tests take" in [Architecture](../architecture.md#testing).
- All the proposals of 0.4.0, P-60 to P-78, are answered, and are D-86 to D-107 in
  [Decisions](../decisions.md). Six were changed by their answers. D-108 changes how a
  row with no serial number is matched. P-79 holds what the review found the decisions
  silent on, and waits for an answer.
- Scenarios S18 to S23 of the [build plan](../build-plan.md) pass as they now read, with
  S01 to S17.
