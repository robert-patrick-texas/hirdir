# hirdir 0.6.0

Money, time and capacity. Support agreements and contracts say what is covered and
until when, purchase requests are received into items, items are registered with the
other tools that know them, and the support weight of the inventory is balanced
against what the team and its outside help can carry.

## New

- **Contracts.** A contract is with a vendor, has a number, a period, a status, a
  support level and an annual cost, and covers one item or many. It is one record:
  each item under it holds the link and nothing else, so what the contract says is
  said once and every item shows it. A contract also says how it was bought and whom
  to ask: the request or tracking number of its present term, the reseller, an
  account manager and a support contact as free text, and where support is reached
  online.
- **Renewing in one place.** A contract that is renewed is given its new number and
  its new period by one change, **Renew** on its page, `POST /contracts/{hid}/renew`
  or `hirdir contracts renew`. Every item in scope has the new number and the new
  period, and nothing is written to any of them. The terms a contract had before,
  each with its number, its period and what it was bought under, are listed beneath
  it as its purchase history.
- **A renewal that is early, and one that is late.** A renewal is often entered
  before the old term ends: one whose period begins after today is kept as the next
  term, the present number and period stay in force until that day, and the items
  are covered throughout. A renewal is also often late, while procurement works and
  the vendor still gives support by escalation: the status `renewal_pending` says so,
  the contract goes on covering past the end of its period, and its items say that
  the account manager is the way in. A contract whose period has ended and that still
  says it is active is flagged as lapsed. On the day a renewed term begins, a
  contract that said expired or renewal pending is active again; one that was
  cancelled stays so.
- **Support agreements.** An agreement with a vendor, such as an enterprise
  agreement, groups contracts. A contract in one has from it whatever the contract
  leaves blank: the reseller, the support level, the account manager, the support
  contact, the support address and the period. Each is shown as inherited, with where
  it came from, and is overridden by saying it on the contract. A contract with no
  dates of its own ends when its agreement does, so a new period on the agreement
  moves every such contract and every item under them by one change. A contract may
  also stand by itself, for switches bought lately, and is rolled into an agreement
  later by one change of that contract.
- **Coverage.** Items are put under a contract several at a time, each for the
  contract's period or for days of its own. A coverage is ended with a date; one that
  was a mistake is removed. Retiring an item, or moving it to Released or Archived,
  ends its coverage that day. An item says whether a contract covers it today, until
  when, and by how many contracts, on its page and in the grid: `covered`,
  `covered_until`, `contract_count` and `contract.number` filter and sort the item
  list.
- **The coverage report.** How many major items are in service and how many of them
  a contract covers, and five lists: what is in service under no contract; what is
  out of service and still counted by a contract, which is the list to send the
  vendor; whose cover runs out within ninety days; what more than one contract
  covers, to look at before one of them is renewed; and what is covered while a
  renewal is pending. Each count leads to its items, in the grid and in an export,
  and the contracts that have lapsed are counted beside them. What "in service" means
  is the `service_states` setting: Assigned, Test, Prod and Down until it is changed,
  so a spare that is parked with the team and under no contract is a gap.
- **The end-of-life report.** The items past, and within two years of, each of the
  four dates, End of Sale, End of Software Support, End of Hardware Support and Last
  Day of Support, and for one of them by fiscal year. The fiscal year starts in the
  month the `fiscal_year_start_month` setting says.
- **The portfolio of a vendor.** Everything from one vendor in one place: its items
  as a whole and for each of the four kinds, hardware, virtual machines, software and
  services, its models with their dates and how many are covered, its agreements, its
  contracts and the capacity lines that name it. `hirdir vendors portfolio` writes
  its items, one row each with their cover, as the working list for a renewal.
- **Purchases.** A purchase request has a reference, such as an RITM, and lines of
  model and quantity, each named `RITM0012345/2`. Nothing of it is inventory.
  Receiving a line says which items arrived, by serial number, asset tag or name, or
  how many arrived that nothing is known of yet, each of which is named for its line,
  `RITM0012345/1-003`, until it has a serial number: a file that names it so gives
  it one. They are created in Delivered, each naming its line.
  A line says how many have arrived and how many are still to come, and never takes
  more than its quantity. A request whose lines have all arrived says that it has
  been received, by itself.
- **External systems and registrations.** A monitoring tool, a configuration backup,
  an SDN controller or a cloud service is a record, and an item or a device group is
  registered with any number of them, each with the other system's key for it. A
  stack that a tool knows as one device is registered as the stack, and each of its
  members shows what the stack is registered with.
  `registration.system:ne:NetWatch` lists what a system does not know.
- **Capacity.** Supply is entered as lines: staff positions by level, professional
  services, smart hands, tools and services. Other work, a project or a transition,
  is entered as demand lines. The capacity report sets the support weight of the
  inventory, turned into resource units, and the other work against the supply:
  demand, supply by category, the balance, the coverage, and how much of the supply
  is the team's own positions. A draft line is a what-if, and the report shows the
  balance without the drafts and with them, side by side.
- **Replacements, reported and in files.** `GET /reports/replacements` lists what was
  replaced by what, when and why, with the count of each reason.
- **Reports by contract.** The summary and the management report take `contract`: an
  item is in the row of each contract that covers it today, and the blank row is what
  no contract covers.
- **In files.** Agreements, contracts, coverage, purchase requests and their lines,
  external systems, registrations, capacity lines and replacements are imported and
  exported as CSV and JSON. A file of items may name the purchase line each arrived
  on, which is how the serial numbers of a delivery are loaded. The examples have two
  agreements with their contracts, a contract by itself, a purchase request with two
  switches arrived, three external systems, the capacity lines of the worked example
  and a replacement, in twenty-five files.
- **On the command line.** `hirdir agreements`, `contracts`, `purchases`, `systems`,
  `registrations` and `capacity` with `list`; `hirdir contracts show`, `cover`, `set`
  and `renew`; `hirdir agreements renew`; `hirdir purchases show` and `receive`;
  `hirdir vendors portfolio`; and
  `hirdir report coverage`, `end-of-life`, `replacements` and `capacity`.

## Changed

- **The record of an item** lists its `coverage`, with `covered`, `covered_until` and
  `contract_count`, its `registrations`, those of the stack or cluster it is in, and
  the `purchase_line` it arrived on, and **the record of a device group** its
  `registrations`.
- **The load order of files.** External systems, agreements, contracts, purchase
  requests and their lines come after the networks and before the items; the
  replacements after the members of groups; and the coverage, the registrations and
  the capacity lines last. See [Examples](../../examples/README.md).

## Additional Notes

- **Upgrading from 0.5.0** adds eight empty tables and one column, and changes
  nothing that is held: see
  [From 0.5.0 to 0.6.0](../install.md#from-050-to-060). No item is under a contract
  until the contracts are entered, so the coverage report begins with every major
  item in service as uncovered, which is true.
- **A coverage that is given the last day of its contract as its end follows the
  contract into a renewal**, as one with no end of its own does. An item that is to
  be left out of a renewal has its coverage ended on an earlier day, or is taken out
  of the contract once that is renewed.
- **Every choice of this release has been answered.** They were asked while it was
  being built, and are D-137 to D-156 in [Decisions](../decisions.md).
- **For those who build `hirdir`:** the server of this release was reviewed twice by
  a reader who had not written it, the second time after the answers were built, and
  the package test carries an inventory of this release's records for the upgrades
  that follow. See "Testing" in [Architecture](../architecture.md#testing).
